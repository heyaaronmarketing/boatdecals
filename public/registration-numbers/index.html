The federal rule, in full. Under 33 CFR 173.27, a registration number must:

  • be painted on or permanently attached to each side of the forward half of the vessel;
  • be in plain vertical block characters of not less than 3 inches in height;
  • contrast with the colour of the background and be distinctly visible and legible;
  • have spaces or hyphens between the letter and number groupings equal to the width of a letter other than “I” or a number other than “1” — so TX 1234 AB, not TX1234AB;
  • read from left to right.
33 CFR § 173.27 — Numbers: Display; size; colour. Applies in every state. Your state can add to it, but cannot drop below it.

Questions people actually ask

Does every state use the same rule?

The core rule is federal and identical everywhere: 3-inch minimum, plain vertical block characters, contrasting colour, spaces or hyphens between groups, reading left to right, on both sides of the forward half. States can add requirements on top but cannot go below it. What genuinely varies is the prefix, the issuing agency, and exactly where the validation decal sits.

Which states do not use their postal abbreviation?

California is the big one - California boat numbers start with CF, not CA, and everyone there calls them CF numbers. Washington uses WN rather than WA. Always copy the prefix off your own registration card rather than assuming.

My state is not listed. Can you still cut my numbers?

Yes. We cut for all 50 states and DC - these are just the pages we have written up so far. Pick 'Other / not listed' on the order form and type your number.
