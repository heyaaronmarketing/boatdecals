Iowa specifics. This is our home state — the shop is in Evansdale, in Black Hawk County. Iowa also says plainly that no other numbers may be displayed on either side of the bow, so strip any old set off before the new one goes on.

What a Iowa number looks like

A Iowa registration number is built from three parts: the state prefix IA, a block of digits, and a letter suffix. Written out with the spacing the law requires, it looks like IA 1234 AB.

Iowa format: IA 3717 ZW — the IA prefix, four digits, then two letters.

Where the validation decal goes in Iowa

Iowa is specific about this: the validation decal goes on each side toward the stern of the registration number, 4 inches from it and in line with it. Not above, not below, not touching.

The federal rule, in full. Under 33 CFR 173.27, a registration number must:

  • be painted on or permanently attached to each side of the forward half of the vessel;
  • be in plain vertical block characters of not less than 3 inches in height;
  • contrast with the color of the background and be distinctly visible and legible;
  • have spaces or hyphens between the letter and number groupings equal to the width of a letter other than “I” or a number other than “1” — so TX 1234 AB, not TX1234AB;
  • read from left to right.
33 CFR § 173.27 — Numbers: Display; size; color. Applies in every state. Your state can add to it, but cannot drop below it.

Getting the number in the first place

Register the boat through your county recorder's office (Iowa registers boats through the county, not directly through the DNR) first. That is where the number and the validation decal come from. We are a vinyl shop, not a registry — we cannot look your number up or issue one, so order once you have the certificate in hand and copy the line straight off it.

The rule most people miss is contrast. There is no required color — but white numbers on a white hull are not “distinctly visible and legible,” and that is a failed inspection. Dark hull, light numbers. Light hull, dark numbers. Our order form flags the combination before it gets cut.

Where people boat in Iowa

Iowa boating splits three ways: the natural glacial lakes in the north-west, the Corps of Engineers reservoirs through the middle, and the two big rivers that form both state lines.

  • Lake Okoboji and Spirit Lakethe Iowa Great Lakes in Dickinson County, and the busiest recreational water in the state. West Okoboji is a true blue-water glacial lake and the summer traffic shows it.
  • Clear Lake3,600 acres in Cerro Gordo County, shallow, windy and a sailing and pontoon lake more than a ski lake.
  • Rathbun Lakearound 11,000 acres near Centerville, the largest lake in southern Iowa and the one people trailer to for a long weekend.
  • Saylorville and Red Rockthe Des Moines River reservoirs, close enough to the metro that they take the weekday evening traffic.
  • Coralville Lakethe eastern Iowa reservoir above Iowa City, with the Corps campgrounds that fill up from May.
  • The Mississippi and the Missouriboth state borders are navigable river. Pool boating on the Mississippi around Dubuque, Guttenberg and the Quad Cities is a whole scene of its own, and the lock system means you share water with commercial tows.

The season. Ice-out is usually late March to mid-April and most boats are out of the water by the end of October. The practical season is about six months, which is why spring re-lettering is such a rush here.

What has to be registered in Iowa

Iowa registers: all watercraft - excludes inflatables under 7 feet and canoes, kayaks and SUPs under 13 feet.

That is the Coast Guard’s own summary of the Iowa DNR system. It matters because it decides whether you need a number on the bow at all — the answer for a kayak or a canoe is different in nearly every state, and a boat that does not need registering does not need numbers from us either. If you are not required to register it, we would rather tell you that than sell you a set.

Iowa questions

How tall do Iowa boat registration numbers have to be?

Not less than 3 inches, in plain vertical block characters. That is the federal minimum under 33 CFR 173.27 and it applies in Iowa like everywhere else. Taller is always legal.

What do Iowa boat numbers start with?

IA. Your full number runs prefix, digits, then a letter suffix — for example IA 1234 AB. Copy it off your registration card exactly rather than guessing the spacing.

Who issues boat registration in Iowa?

You register through your county recorder's office (Iowa registers boats through the county, not directly through the DNR). We only make the decals — you get the number from them first, then we cut it.

Do the numbers need a space or a hyphen?

Yes. The rule requires a space or hyphen between the letter and number groups, about as wide as one character. So IA 1234 AB, not IA1234AB. Ours arrive pre-spaced correctly, so you cannot get this wrong.

Where do they go on the boat?

Each side of the forward half of the hull, reading left to right, in a color that contrasts with the hull. Iowa is specific about this: the validation decal goes on each side toward the stern of the registration number, 4 inches from it and in line with it. Not above, not below, not touching.

Do I have to register a kayak or canoe in Iowa?

Iowa registers all watercraft - excludes inflatables under 7 feet and canoes, kayaks and SUPs under 13 feet. If your boat falls outside that, it does not need a registration number on the bow — and it does not need a set from us either. This is the Coast Guard's own summary of the Iowa DNR system.

How many registered boats are there in Iowa?

220,207, which puts Iowa 18th in the country. That is the Coast Guard's 2024 count, from Recreational Boating Statistics, Table 38.

Where are the best places to boat in Iowa?

Lake Okoboji and Spirit Lake is the one most people name first: the Iowa Great Lakes in Dickinson County, and the busiest recreational water in the state. West Okoboji is a true blue-water glacial lake and the summer traffic shows it. Others worth knowing: Clear Lake, Rathbun Lake, Saylorville and Red Rock, Coralville Lake.
