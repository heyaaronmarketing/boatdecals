What a Ohio number looks like

A Ohio registration number is built from three parts: the state prefix OH, a block of digits, and a letter suffix. Written out with the spacing the law requires, it looks like OH 1234 AB.

The federal rule, in full. Under 33 CFR 173.27, a registration number must:

  • be painted on or permanently attached to each side of the forward half of the vessel;
  • be in plain vertical block characters of not less than 3 inches in height;
  • contrast with the colour of the background and be distinctly visible and legible;
  • have spaces or hyphens between the letter and number groupings equal to the width of a letter other than “I” or a number other than “1” — so TX 1234 AB, not TX1234AB;
  • read from left to right.
33 CFR § 173.27 — Numbers: Display; size; colour. Applies in every state. Your state can add to it, but cannot drop below it.

Getting the number in the first place

Register the boat through the Ohio DNR Division of Parks and Watercraft first. That is where the number and the validation decal come from. We are a vinyl shop, not a registry — we cannot look your number up or issue one, so order once you have the certificate in hand and copy the line straight off it.

The rule most people miss is contrast. There is no required colour — but white numbers on a white hull are not “distinctly visible and legible,” and that is a failed inspection. Dark hull, light numbers. Light hull, dark numbers. Our order form flags the combination before it gets cut.

Where people boat in Ohio

Ohio registers all watercraft, which is why its fleet sits so high in the national count - kayaks and canoes are in the number here.

  • Lake Eriethe shallowest and warmest Great Lake, which makes it both the most productive and the quickest to turn dangerous. The Western Basin around Port Clinton and the Bass Islands is the busiest.
  • Grand Lake St. Marys13,500 acres, built as a canal feeder and still the largest inland lake in the state.
  • Mosquito Creek and Pymatuningthe north-east reservoirs, with Pymatuning shared with Pennsylvania.
  • Alum Creek and Hoover Reservoirthe Columbus weekday lakes.
  • Indian Lakea shallow 5,800-acre lake in Logan County with a long resort history.
  • The Ohio Riverthe whole southern border, locked and navigable, shared with barge traffic.

The season. Roughly late April through October. Lake Erie's walleye run pulls boats out early, often before the weather deserves it.

Ohio registers: all watercraft.

Ohio questions

How tall do Ohio boat registration numbers have to be?

Not less than 3 inches, in plain vertical block characters. That is the federal minimum under 33 CFR 173.27 and it applies in Ohio like everywhere else. Taller is always legal.

What do Ohio boat numbers start with?

OH. Your full number runs prefix, digits, then a letter suffix — for example OH 1234 AB. Copy it off your registration card exactly rather than guessing the spacing.

Who issues boat registration in Ohio?

You register through the Ohio DNR Division of Parks and Watercraft. We only make the decals — you get the number from them first, then we cut it.

Do the numbers need a space or a hyphen?

Yes. The rule requires a space or hyphen between the letter and number groups, about as wide as one character. So OH 1234 AB, not OH1234AB. Ours arrive pre-spaced correctly, so you cannot get this wrong.

Where do they go on the boat?

Each side of the forward half of the hull, reading left to right, in a colour that contrasts with the hull. The validation decal goes near the number — Ohio publishes the exact offset, and it is usually within a few inches.

Do I have to register a kayak or canoe in Ohio?

Ohio registers all watercraft. If your boat falls outside that, it does not need a registration number on the bow — and it does not need a set from us either. This is the Coast Guard's own summary of the Ohio DNR Division of Parks and Watercraft system.

How many registered boats are there in Ohio?

630,288, which puts Ohio fourth in the country. That is the Coast Guard's 2024 count, from Recreational Boating Statistics, Table 38.

Where are the best places to boat in Ohio?

Lake Erie is the one most people name first: the shallowest and warmest Great Lake, which makes it both the most productive and the quickest to turn dangerous. The Western Basin around Port Clinton and the Bass Islands is the busiest. Others worth knowing: Grand Lake St. Marys, Mosquito Creek and Pymatuning, Alum Creek and Hoover Reservoir, Indian Lake.

When is boating season in Ohio?

Roughly late April through October. Lake Erie's walleye run pulls boats out early, often before the weather deserves it.
