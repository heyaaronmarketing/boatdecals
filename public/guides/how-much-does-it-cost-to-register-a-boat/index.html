How Much Does It Cost to Register a Boat?

Short answer: across the 51 states we have verified, the published figures run from $5.50 to $448 — and 18 of them register a boat for three years at a time rather than annually. The fee is set by your state, almost always by the boat’s length, and it is a different thing from what the numbers on the bow cost.

Read the term, not just the number

This is where most comparisons go wrong. A $60 registration in one state and a $60 registration in the next are not the same price if one runs three years and the other runs twelve months. Of the states above, most register a boat for three years, a few for two, and a handful annually — so the honest comparison is cost per year, and the gap between states is usually smaller than the headline suggests.

What else lands on the bill

The registration fee is rarely the whole invoice. The usual additions:

  • A title fee. Iowa adds $13.50 for a boat 17 feet or longer. Illinois requires a title on anything over 21 feet, as a separate transaction people miss.
  • A processing or agent fee. Missouri adds $9 to every transaction, Illinois $2, and Pennsylvania’s issuing agents add their own on top.
  • Excise or use tax. A separate charge with its own logic. Indiana is the clearest case: its annual boat excise comes from the boat’s original retail price and its age, not its length, so a twenty-year-old boat that was expensive when new still sits in a high band.
  • A late penalty. Iowa adds $5, and renewing by post adds another $2.

The quirks that catch people out

These are real, they are in each state’s own schedule, and they are the reason a quote from a national chart is often wrong:

  • Pontoons are the exception to Michigan's whole length-based table: $23 flat at any length. A 24-foot pontoon pays $23 where a 24-foot runabout pays $115.
  • Georgia expires registrations on the owner's BIRTH MONTH, not a fixed date - so no two boats on the same dock come due together.
  • Every Washington decal expires on 30 JUNE regardless of when you registered, so a boat bought in May gets about six weeks before its first renewal.
  • Wisconsin's registration year starts on 1 April, not 1 January - a boat registered in March gets a very short first year.
  • Louisiana's bands break at 15 ft 11 in and 25 ft 11 in rather than on the round foot - measure before you assume which band a 16-foot boat is in.
  • South Carolina moved from $30 every three years to $10 every year - the same money, a different rhythm - and the renewal arrives with the county tax bill, which is why people miss it.
  • Iowa prices a NEW registration lower than a renewal, and the new-registration price steps down as the three-year cycle runs - so what you pay depends on when in the cycle you buy the boat.

Four states that do not publish a figure

We would rather say so than print a number we cannot stand behind:

  • North Carolina — the NCWRC publishes no fee on its registration page.
  • Washington — the DOL publishes a $10.50 base and tells boaters to use its own calculator for the real total.
  • Indiana — there is no length table to print: the bill is a length fee plus an excise class set by original price and age.
  • Alabama — two versions of ALEA’s own form carry different tables, and neither reads cleanly enough for us to be sure which governs.

Each of those state pages says the same thing in plain words and points you at the agency that can tell you.

Who you pay, and what we have nothing to do with

You pay the state, directly, and in several states that means a county office rather than a state one — Iowa registers through the county recorder, Alabama through county licensing offices, Missouri through the Department of Revenue rather than the Highway Patrol. Nobody can register a boat on your behalf, and anyone offering to is adding a fee to something you can do yourself.

What the state gives you is the number — a line on a card. Getting it onto the bow in 3-inch block characters that meet the federal display rule is the part that is yours, and the part we do. If you are costing that up rather than the registration, our guide to what number decals cost covers it, and our pricing is published.

If your state is not in the table

We add a state when we have read its figures on its own site, not before. If yours is missing it means we have not verified it yet — not that it is free. Your state’s agency is the authority, and every state page we have links straight to it.

Questions people actually ask

How much does it cost to register a boat?

In most states, between about $20 and $100 for a boat under 26 feet, and the registration usually runs one to three years rather than annually. The figure is set by your state and almost always by the boat's LENGTH - so a 14-foot jon boat and a 40-foot cruiser pay very different amounts in the same state, and most states register for two or three years rather than annually. The table on this page carries each state's own published figures, with the date we last checked them.

Why is registration so much cheaper in my friend's state?

Because states price it differently and the term differs too. South Carolina collects a small annual fee through the county tax bill, while Michigan runs from a low double-digit figure for the smallest boats to the high hundreds for the largest. Comparing a one-year fee in one state against a three-year fee in another makes the gap look bigger than it is - check the term as well as the number.

Is the fee annual?

Not usually. Most states registered a boat for THREE years, a few for two, and a handful annually. The table on this page gives the term for every state we have verified, because a $60 three-year registration and a $60 annual one are not the same price.

Does the registration fee include the numbers for the hull?

No. The state issues you the NUMBER - the characters themselves, as a line on a card or a certificate. Putting them on the boat is yours to do, and that is what we sell: a legal, pre-spaced set for both sides, shipped free. The current price is on our pricing page.

Why does my state charge by boat length?

It is a rough proxy for what the boat costs the state: bigger boats use more ramp, patrol and waterway resource. A few states break that pattern - Michigan prices every pontoon at one flat rate whatever its length, which works out cheaper than the smallest runabout band. Our Michigan page carries the figure.

What else is on the bill besides the registration fee?

Depending on the state: a title fee (Iowa adds $13.50 for a boat 17 feet or longer), a processing or agent fee (Missouri $9, Illinois $2), a late penalty, and in some states a separate excise or use tax that has nothing to do with length. Indiana sets its annual excise from the boat's ORIGINAL retail price and its age, so an old boat that was expensive when new still pays a high band.

Which states do not publish a registration fee?

Four of the states we have checked do not put a usable figure on their own site: North Carolina publishes none on its registration page; Washington publishes a $10.50 base and tells boaters to use its calculator; Indiana prices by excise class rather than length; and Alabama's own form circulates in two versions with different tables. We say so on those pages rather than printing a number we cannot stand behind.

When does my registration expire?

It varies, and not always from the date you registered. Georgia expires at the end of the owner's BIRTH MONTH. Washington expires 30 June for everyone, so a boat registered in May gets about six weeks before its first renewal. Wisconsin's year starts 1 April. Iowa's current three-year cycle ends 30 April 2028.

Do I pay you or the state?

The state, always, and not through us. We are a vinyl shop - we cut and ship the characters. Nobody can register your boat for you except your state's agency, and anyone who offers to is adding a fee to something you can do directly.

Where do these figures come from?

Each state's own agency - the DNR, the department of revenue, the wildlife agency or the licensing department, whichever one issues the number - and every state page links the exact page we read it on, with the date we last checked it. Where two state pages disagreed we went to the primary fee schedule and said so.

Is the fee different for a kayak or a canoe?

In most states an unpowered kayak or canoe does not need registering at all. Pennsylvania is the clear exception - it registers unpowered boats too, and a kayak needs either a registration or a launch permit, which costs the same. Our Pennsylvania page carries the current figure, and each state page says what that state actually requires.

All 50 states and DC, free. We cut for every state's registration number format, email you a proof, and post it once you approve.
