# Image captioning rules

These images are **AI-generated illustrative product shots**. They show what
our lettering looks like applied to a hull. They are not photographs of real
customers' boats.

## The rule

**Never caption a generated image as a named person's boat.**

❌ `Lettering from Dave, MI`
❌ `Customer photo — Sea-Doo RXP`
❌ `Here's what Jim's turned out like`

✅ `MC 1234 AB · 3" black on a white hull`
✅ `White on navy · bow, both sides`
✅ `Transom name · 6" script`

The caption describes **the spec**, not a person. That is honest, and it is
better merchandising anyway: a buyer reading `3" black on a white hull` is
reading the exact order they are about to place.

## Why this matters more than it looks

The reference gallery we're modelling (boatdecals.com/Gallery) runs to 161
pages of genuinely customer-submitted photos captioned with real first names
and states. Its entire value is that it is real. Imitating the captions rather
than the photography turns an illustration into fabricated social proof - the
same category as a fake review, and the one mistake that cannot be quietly
undone once a customer spots their "own" boat.

Industry numbers behind the house rule: Getty VisualGPS found 98% of consumers
say authentic images are pivotal to trust; Clutch found ~40% would trust a
brand *less* for undisclosed AI imagery.

From `~/.claude/skills/tradephoto/SKILL.md`:

> Never present an AI image as a specific customer's job.

## Where disclosure goes

- **Product and guide pages** — a single line in the page footer area:
  *"Illustrative images. Lettering shown is representative of what we cut."*
- **Social posts** — platform AI-content label ON, per the `social-media-content`
  skill's mandatory-disclosure rule.
- **A future real gallery** — see below. Real photos need no disclaimer, which
  is the point.

## The replacement plan

Every order ships in an envelope. Put a card in it:

> *Send us a photo of it on the boat and we'll take $5 off your next set.*

Within a season that is a real gallery with real names, and these generated
shots get demoted to hero/atmosphere only. AI is the bridge, not the
destination - the sites that win this category long-term are the ones with 161
pages of real customers.
